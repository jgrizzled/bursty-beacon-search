## 2026-08-17 — FRB 20200120E exploratory analyses (recorded before the M5 stage-1 run) and reporting consequences

Context: M4 stage-1 calibration is complete (FAP_M4 = 0/1000, `phase1/calibration_results/M4/RUN_NOTES.md`); the frozen order continues with M5. The analyses below were run and committed **before** any M5 simulation, so they cannot be post-hoc rationalization of a stage-1 outcome. Both are **exploratory and non-confirmatory**: analysis A is a diagnostic decomposition of already-computed frozen scan output; analysis B uses CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 data, which is outside the frozen sample (its public exposure product is integrated-only HEALPix, failing the S1 window-completeness criterion, exactly as anticipated by the prereg scope note). Neither analysis alters any frozen input, grid, statistic, or the stage-1 procedure. Reproduce both with `uv run python scripts/exploratory_20200120E_chime.py`.

### A. The Λ*-driving fit is a two-cluster artifact, not an identified periodicity

Decomposition of the frozen `effelsberg20200120E` scan output (`phase1/confirmatory_results.json`):

- **Every one of the twelve Λ-plateau peaks (Δll within ~2 of the maximum) has a period that is an integer subharmonic of 62.2444 d** — the separation between the first burst of the 53-burst N31 storm (MJD 59593.709) and the N38 burst pair (MJD 59655.953): T = 62.21, 62.08, 31.11, 20.73, 12.44, 8.88, 7.78, 4.79, 2.96, 2.22 d ↔ n = 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 13, 21, 28, all to within 0.05–0.3% (residuals absorbed by t₀). One interval between two burst clusters generates the entire period family; the period parameter is unidentified.
- **Every peak's visits contain the same 53 events**: the storm's densest 51-burst, 32-min core plus the N38 double (two bursts 0.8 min apart). The remaining 16 bursts — 7 of the 9 burst-bearing sessions, including all four Pearlman-epoch detections and B6–B9 — are relegated to the free Poisson background (fitted λ_v ≈ 1,600–3,000 d⁻¹ vs λ_b ≈ 2.9 d⁻¹).
- **The strict schedule is untested beyond the two anchors**: only ~25 min of the 5.57 live days falls inside visits, and for the n = 1 period only 2 of its 8 visit epochs in the baseline have any exposure. Λ* = 86.78 is generated by (a) concentrating 51 bursts into a 32-min box inside a 120-min session — a within-session structure none of M0/M1/M4/M5 can represent (M1's duty floor is δ ≥ 0.05 and its fit pins there; M5 is uniform within session) — plus (b) one pair 62.24 d later, with periods selected to thread the exposure gaps.

This makes the manifest-1.7a storm-fitting caveat exact: the statistic detects "one storm + one pair," and its calibrated rarity under the frozen nulls measures family misspecification, not beacon-like structure.

### B. CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 out-of-sample fold refutes the leading surviving peaks

Data: `~/chimefrb/catalog2/table/chimefrbcat2.csv` (sha256 `5108ada779d279a2547d9f9e73ae25bfdd40d8496d6ba7255ec29c6629057a48`), rows with `repeater_name = FRB20200120E`: 7 bursts, MJD 58868–59449 (catalog span extends to MJD 60202). The `excluded_flag = 1` discovery-day burst is dropped; the 6 remaining topocentric infinite-frequency TOAs are barycentered through the frozen `phase1/bary.py` path (DE440, CHIME site, frozen VLBI position). Because the non-alias peak periods are non-day-commensurate by construction, CHIME's transit exposure is phase-uniform over the ~1,878-d catalog span, so the fitted model predicts the in-visit probability per CHIME detection from λ_v·duty vs λ_b. Folding the 6 TOAs through each would-be-candidate peak's fitted (T, σ_v, τ_c, t₀):

| Peak | duty | P(in-visit) predicted | observed | P(0 of 6) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8.88 d M3 (top surviving runner-up) | 1.00% | 0.91 | 0/6 | 4.0×10⁻⁷ |
| 20.73 d M3 | 0.43% | 0.71 | 0/6 | 6.4×10⁻⁴ |
| 31.11 d M3 | 0.14% | 0.60 | 0/6 | 4.1×10⁻³ |

| 62.21 d M2 | 0.04% | 0.27 | 0/6 | 1.5×10⁻¹ |

The two strongest surviving peaks are refuted; the n = 1, 2 subharmonics are disfavored to unconstrained. Caveats, stated with the result: the test assumes achromatic visits (CHIME 400–800 MHz vs Effelsberg L-band; a sweeping beacon should be broadband, and chromatic activity windows are themselves a documented *natural* repeater behavior) and that in-visit and background bursts share a fluence distribution above CHIME's threshold.

Additionally, the CHIME detections at MJD 59325.17 / 59327.16 / 59334.14 bracket the Effelsberg P12 (59332.50) and P13 (59335.05–59335.20) detections: six detection epochs in ten days across two instruments and two bands — a week-scale correlated **active episode**. No member of the frozen natural family can produce this process (M5 draws session rates i.i.d.; M4 is a renewal process; M1's duty is ≥ 5%), which is the concrete sense in which the stage-1 FAPs answer "how often do these four nulls fake Λ ≥ Λ*," not "how often does nature."

### H1 conclusion framing (binding for any v1.0 report)

The M2/M3 vs M0/M1/M4/M5 comparison discriminates *narrow-duty strict periodicity* from *broad-duty or aperiodic* natural behavior — it cannot discriminate an artificial beacon from a natural narrow-duty periodic emitter (rotation, precession, or orbital modulation of a compact object), because such a source is itself a generator of the M2/M3 class and the natural family contains no narrow-duty periodic member. The Section 7 contamination check covers catalogued *Galactic* pulsars/RRATs only and cannot exclude an uncatalogued compact object in the M81 globular cluster, which is the mainstream interpretation of this source. Accordingly, **even a fully calibrated FAP < 10⁻³ candidate under this prereg would be reported as an unexplained strict-periodicity timing anomaly — "natural clock or beacon," with the natural clock as the default reading — never as evidence of artificiality.** Artificiality discriminators are follow-up work outside H1: unique period identification supported by ≥ 3 exposed visit epochs, secular period stability inconsistent with spin-down/precession wander, achromatic visit times, fixed-time-lag vs fixed-phase pair structure, and the H2 inner-structure tests.

## Data-handling notes (post-tag, prereg-h1-v1.0; no frozen procedure changed)

Recorded per the S2 "within verified windows" criterion and the prereg 2.2 conversion rules as frozen; these are documented applications of frozen rules to published-data inconsistencies discovered at first confirmatory data contact (2026-08-09), not deviations. Full machine-readable detail is in `phase1/confirmatory_results.json` (per-campaign `events` / `windows.containment` blocks) and the `EDGE_TOL_S` comment in `phase1/confirmatory.py`.

1. **Nançay 20220912A TOA frame correction.** The `Supplementary Material (online).csv` column `Time of arrival (MJD)` is empirically **topocentric**, not barycentric TDB as annotated in `phase1/CONFIRMATORY_PLAN.md`: 639/640 merged events lie inside the topocentric session log (whose start values equal the candidate-file filterbank `tstart`s), while the barycentric reading places 84/640 outside the converted windows. Handled by the prereg 2.2 frozen path for topocentric inputs (topo UTC → BJD_TDB, DE440, Nançay site, frozen position). Residual reference-frequency ambiguity ≤ ~1 s ≪ σ_v,min = 60 s.
2. **Event exclusions under S2 "within verified windows"** (published burst table vs published session log inconsistencies; keeping such events would also bias Λ, since phase-folded models count them while the per-session M4/M5 likelihoods skip them):
   - Nançay **B602** (TOA 59900.827, the table's only 3-decimal entry): ≥ 160 s after its same-day session end on either time axis.
   - AstroFlash **B39-st, B67-st**: single-station Stockert rows ~5,000 s outside every logged scan of any station, in both the reduced 508-session log and the 5,842-row per-scan log. (Observation only: a −2 h shift would place both inside Stockert scans, but this does not match CET for the November burst; no repair applied.)
3. **Session-edge tolerance (30 s) for the prereg 4.1 containment check.** Two documented convention mismatches: (a) infinite-frequency TOAs can precede converted session starts by the intra-band dispersion sweep (~2.2 s max across the sample); (b) the FAST 20240114A release's published stop times end before its own last burst TOAs in ~33 sessions by up to 23.6 s (Zhou 111-row and Zhang 57-row logs agree to ~1 s, so this is nominal-vs-actual recording stop in the release). Events within 30 s (≪ σ_v,min = 60 s) are kept at their published times and reported (59 such events for FAST 20240114A, 1 for FAST 20201124A Sep–Oct); the frozen windows and TOAs are unmodified. Beyond 30 s, the S2 exclusion above applies, guarded by a max(3, 1%) abort against systematic frame errors.
